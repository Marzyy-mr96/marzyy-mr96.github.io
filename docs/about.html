About

Connecting data, AI, and disaster resilience

I’m Marzieh Allahyari, a Ph.D. researcher in Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Kansas, advised by Dr. Elaina J. Sutley. I study long-term housing recovery after disasters, with particular attention to how repeated flooding affects households already rebuilding. My work brings engineering judgment and hands-on data analysis to questions about community resilience and unequal recovery.

My background in structural engineering taught me to work carefully with models and uncertainty. During my master’s research at Tarbiat Modares University, I developed computational tools to study how uncertain material properties and dimensions affect reinforced concrete structures. At Kansas, I have carried that perspective into the NIST-funded longitudinal study of Lumberton, North Carolina, following housing recovery after Hurricanes Matthew and Florence.

Working with observations collected over several years has shown me how closely reliable analysis depends on the quality of the underlying records. I reconcile information across survey waves, resolve inconsistencies, and document how missing or changing responses are handled. My current manuscripts build on that foundation through sensitivity analysis, comparisons of recovery over time, and examination of demographic differences. Tables, visualizations, and clear explanations are central to how I communicate this work.

To look beyond average trends, I use time-series clustering with Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) and latent class growth analysis (LCGA) to identify groups with different recovery pathways. Regression, regularized multinomial models, and linear mixed-effects models help me examine relationships between recovery and household characteristics while accounting for repeated observations. These applications connect my experience in data analytics and data science with an interest in AI, particularly interpretable machine learning that helps answer a research question without overstating what the evidence supports.

I have also developed Recovery KPI Studio, an interactive website built with Streamlit and Plotly that brings data inspection, indicator definition, longitudinal exploration, and interactive visualization into one workspace. Users can work with CSV and Excel files, define equations, and export results alongside their methodology. The project gives me a practical way to translate analytical needs into software that makes calculations and assumptions easier to inspect.

Alongside my research and software projects, I am developing my data engineering knowledge through completed Udemy courses in data integration and modern data warehousing, complemented by training in data analysis and generative AI. This learning extends the perspective I bring to organizing and using complex data. I am interested in opportunities across industry, research institutions, and academia where I can contribute to dependable data, thoughtful modeling, and useful analytical tools, within disaster resilience and beyond.
